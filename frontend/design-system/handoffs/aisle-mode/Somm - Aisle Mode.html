Aisle mode — one window, two faces
Considered mode stays the front door and the primary experience: it's for people who don't know what to get. Aisle mode is the same window turned sideways — no sliders, no chips, one question — for people already standing in a store. Three approaches to the switch below; I argue for the third. The considered face shown in each is the real PreferenceCapture screen — style grid, budget ceiling and occasion pills, cropped to fit; wine type, free text and zip continue below the fold.

C · Two faces of one window — recommended. A horizontal swipe with a labelled rail: Plan a bottle ↔ In a store. Spatial, not a setting — you always know where you are because you moved there. Considered stays page one and the default; zip, store and thread carry across. Costs a pager and a gesture affordance; needs the labels to be tappable too, for reachability.
Why C
Same window, turned sideways.
Your two modes aren't two products — they're two postures toward the same question. A swipe says that; a toggle doesn't. Page one stays the structured flow because that's who most sessions are.
The rail labels double as tap targets, so it's reachable one-handed and discoverable without the gesture. And because it's one window, the session's zip and chosen store travel with you — no re-asking.
The one risk
Swipe-only discovery is invisible. Mitigation: on first launch after this ships, the rail's second label pulses once and a one-line hint sits under it — "In a store? Swipe over and just ask." Shown once, never again.
1b · Making C discoverable
You're right that a bare swipe teaches nothing. But the fix isn't to abandon the spatial model — it's to stop making the gesture the only way to learn it. Three iterations, each borrowing a different amount of A's explicitness. The swipe keeps working in all of them; it just stops being the only door.

C-iii · Standing invitation. A persistent bordeaux-tint strip above the tab bar that names the moment in plain language and taps straight over. Discovered by reading, not gesturing — and it sells the mode rather than just exposing it. Cost: permanent furniture in the primary flow; could dismiss after first use.
Recommendation
C-i plus C-iii — two doors.
Tabs make the two faces visible at all times — that alone solves your worry, and it's what you liked about A. The difference from A is purely typographic: labels sharing the header rule instead of a boxed segment, so it reads as two pages rather than a setting.
The two entries then carry different depths of context, which is the real insight. The Ask tab is general — it opens clean and never front-loads a location form, because "is Nebbiolo like Pinot?" doesn't need a zip. The strip is the store-specific door: tapping it means "I'm here, now," so asking which store is welcome rather than friction.
Location is therefore lazy in Ask, eager from the strip. Same face, same thread — just a different amount known on arrival.
On C-ii
The peek is the most elegant teacher but the worst citizen — it taxes 30px from the flow that matters most, permanently, to teach something once. Skip it.
If you'd rather just take A
A with the invitation strip is also a fine ship. You lose the "one window" feeling but keep every practical benefit. The strip is the piece I'd fight for either way.

Build notes
What this assumes.
Rate limit
Assumes the limit is raised — no throttle UI anywhere. Follow-ups are the point of a thread; a visible counter would poison it. Partial-retry above helps by not re-spending a request on a whole answer.
Two-bottle compare
Designed as if it works. Needs the backend to capture two bottle names per query — known work, and the single highest-value fix for this mode.
Grounded in source
Tokens and semantic classes come from design-system/colors_and_type.css; chat grammar, mark, tab bar, loader, store pill and the considered-mode controls match frontend/src on main — not redrawn. Two faces live inside the existing Recommend tab (/ = Plan, /recommend = Ask).
Store pill
Always ◎ store · distance, exactly as today. No shelf or aisle data is assumed anywhere in this design.
Budget
No default range in this face. Suppress "outside your budget" phrasing entirely unless a budget was stated in the sentence.
Camera
Dormant seam beside the context chips. Nothing here depends on it.
